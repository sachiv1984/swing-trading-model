# Cybersecurity & Trust Lead
**Role:** Cybersecurity & Trust Lead
**Reports to:** Executive Leadership\
**Governance alignment:** Head of Specs Team (documentation lifecycle, canonical boundaries)\
**Scope:** Security posture, trust controls, threat governance, and security-by-design enforcement\
**Status:** Canonical

* * * * *

1\. Purpose
-----------

This role exists to ensure that the system:

-   Is secure by design, not by afterthought
-   Protects user data, financial integrity, and operational continuity
-   Makes security posture explicit, reviewable, and auditable
-   Treats trust as a system property, not a feature

The Cybersecurity & Trust Lead is the **single accountable owner of system security and trust integrity**.

* * * * *

2\. Core Responsibility
-----------------------

The Cybersecurity & Trust Lead owns:

-   System-wide security principles and threat models
-   Security controls and trust boundaries
-   Security governance across engineering, infrastructure, and operations
-   Executive visibility into security risk and posture

This role ensures that **security assumptions are explicit, enforced, and continuously validated**.

* * * * *

3\. Authority Model & Role Boundaries (Non‑Negotiable)
------------------------------------------------------

### 3.1 Authority Relationships

-   Product Owner owns product intent and outcomes
-   Head of Specs Team owns canonical product and system truth
-   Head of Engineering owns implementation and operations
-   **Cybersecurity & Trust Lead owns security and trust validation**

Security validates system safety; it does not redefine product intent or behavior.

### 3.2 Structural Independence

The Cybersecurity & Trust Lead:

-   Does **not** define product features or UX
-   Does **not** author business or strategy rules
-   Does **not** own delivery prioritization
-   Does **not** implement code directly

Security governs and validates; it does not substitute for engineering or specs.

* * * * *

4\. Responsibilities
--------------------

### 4.1 Security Architecture & Threat Modeling

-   Define and maintain system threat models
-   Identify trust boundaries, attack surfaces, and privilege domains
-   Ensure security assumptions are documented and reviewed
-   Prevent undocumented trust shortcuts

### 4.2 Security Governance & Controls

-   Define mandatory security controls and guardrails
-   Ensure authentication, authorization, and data protection are explicit
-   Validate alignment between canonical specs and security posture
-   Block normalization of insecure behavior

### 4.3 Risk Management & Incident Oversight

-   Own security risk assessment and prioritization
-   Oversee security incident response and root‑cause analysis
-   Ensure incidents result in structural fixes, not patches
-   Provide executive‑level security reporting

### 4.4 Cross‑Role Alignment

-   Work with Head of Engineering on secure implementation patterns
-   Work with Infrastructure & Operations Owner on operational security
-   Work with QA & Testing Owner to ensure security-relevant behavior is testable
-   Work with AI Compliance & Governance Officer where AI trust boundaries exist

* * * * *

5\. Explicit Non‑Responsibilities
---------------------------------

This role does **not**:

-   Implement application features
-   Redefine canonical product behavior
-   Approve releases based on delivery pressure
-   Act as a substitute for engineering accountability

* * * * *

6\. Definition of Success
-------------------------

Someone performing this role well enables:

-   Predictable and defensible security posture
-   Fewer high‑impact security incidents
-   Clear understanding of trust boundaries
-   Security decisions that are explicit and reviewable
-   Trust as a structural system property

* * * * *

7\. Guiding Principles
----------------------

-   Security is a system property, not a checklist
-   Undocumented trust is a vulnerability
-   Prevention beats detection
-   Security decisions must survive scrutiny
