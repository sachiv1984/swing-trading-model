# Idea: Defect Age Reporting

## 1. Problem Statement

There is no automated or recurring check on how long open defects have been outstanding. A defect raised in a sprint could remain unassigned or unresolved for weeks without any visibility to the Director of Quality or the wider team. The defect_lifecycle.md defines escalation thresholds based on elapsed time — but those thresholds can only be enforced if someone is actively monitoring defect age. Currently, defect age monitoring is either manual or absent.

## 2. Strategic Alignment

Section reference: Director of Quality §4.3 — "govern the full defect lifecycle; the Director of Quality is the final sign-off authority on every verification report; sign-off independence is non-negotiable"

Alignment rationale: Sign-off independence depends on the Director of Quality having visibility into the full defect state — including which defects are aging beyond their escalation threshold. Without defect age reporting, the Director of Quality is dependent on the QA Lead proactively surfacing aging defects, rather than having independent visibility. The defect age report provides that independent visibility.

## 3. Proposed Solution

Implement a weekly GitHub Issues query (filtered by the "defect" label) that generates a sorted report of open defects by age, grouped by severity. Route the report to the QA Lead and Director of Quality. For any defect exceeding the escalation threshold defined in defect_lifecycle.md, add an automatic "escalation-due" label and post a comment tagging the Director of Quality. The report can be generated as a CI scheduled job or a simple script run weekly.

## 4. Expected Value

Prevents defect aging beyond escalation thresholds. Ensures the Director of Quality has independent visibility into defect state without relying on the QA Lead's proactive reporting. Measurable as: number of defects per cycle that exceed their escalation threshold (target: zero, down from unknown current rate).
